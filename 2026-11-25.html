1. JWST/NIRSpec observations of AMORE6 suggest active Pop~III star formation in a self-polluted galaxy

Mirko Curti, Elka Rusta, Stefania Salvadori, Stefano Carniani, Francesco D'Eugenio, Bianca Moreschini et al. · astro-ph.GA · arXiv · PDF · 💻 code/data

Why it was picked: JWST/NIRSpec deep spectroscopy inferring physical properties (abundances, stellar populations) of a high-z galaxy, though focused on Pop III/metal-free chemistry rather than SFH. — score 5.8 (you 5/10 · field 8/10)

TL;DR. Deep JWST/NIRSpec spectroscopy of the strongly lensed z=5.725 galaxy AMORE6-B confirms it as the most metal-poor galaxy known (12+log(O/H)≈6.08) and reveals an exceptionally hard ionizing spectrum (strong He II, weak/no metal lines) that Bayesian photoionization modeling favors attributing to metal-free Population III stars with a top-heavy IMF in a self-polluted phase, though a metal-poor population with a luminous hard X-ray source cannot be statistically excluded from the emission lines alone [Abstract, p.1].

Main question

AMORE6 had previously been flagged, from NIRCam slitless spectroscopy showing no detectable [O III]λ5007, as a candidate for metal-free (Population III) star formation, with an oxygen-abundance limit of 12+log(O/H)<5.97 [p.3]. But that inference rested on a single line ratio and a fixed, specific photoionization model (fixed ionization parameter, density, stellar population age), and weak [O III] can also arise from high density, low ionization parameter, a different ionizing spectrum, or photon leakage rather than from truly pristine gas [p.2]. The paper's central question is "whether deep, multi-line spectroscopy confirms that AMORE6 hosts genuinely near-pristine gas, or whether unusual density, ionization, geometry, or a non-standard ionizing source can account for its spectrum" [p.2]. This matters because confirmed Pop III systems would be a direct probe of the first stars, the primordial IMF, and early chemical enrichment [p.1-2].

Data & method

AMORE6-B is one of two lensed images (µ≃78) of a galaxy behind Abell 2744, with intrinsic \(M_{UV}\simeq-14.5\) and stellar mass \(\sim4\times10^5\,M_\odot\) [p.3]. The authors obtained JWST/NIRSpec multi-object spectroscopy (GO 9645) with gratings G140M, G235M, and G395M (R∼1000), covering rest-frame ~0.14–0.78 µm, with exposure times of 182.9, 46.2, and 50.1 ks respectively [Tab. 3, p.8]. This gives simultaneous access to He IIλ1640/4686, UV C/N/O lines, [O II], [Ne III], the Balmer series, and the auroral [O III]λ4363 — far more diagnostic leverage than the earlier slitless data. Careful work goes into morphology/photometry modeling, slit-loss and path-loss corrections, and an extended-range calibration of G235M to recover Hβ and [O III] (which fall in a detector gap) [§3-4, pp.6-9]. Gas properties (attenuation, temperature, density, helium abundance) are derived by jointly fitting H and He recombination lines [§6.1, p.12]. The core analysis compares the full emission-line spectrum against Cloudy photoionization grids for six physically distinct families of ionizing sources — self-consistent Pop III simulations (NEFERTITI), Pop III population-synthesis models with different IMFs, ordinary extremely metal-poor stellar populations, very massive stars, X-ray binaries, and massive black holes/AGN — ranked by Bayesian evidence [Tab. 5, p.20; §7.4, p.22]. A full SED fit (bagpipes) is then performed under the favored scenarios [§8, p.23].

Main results

  1. AMORE6-B shows one of the hardest ionizing spectra known: He IIλ4686/Hβ=0.080±0.015, EW(He IIλ4686)=35±8 Å, with weak [O III] (R3=0.249±0.025) and detected C IVλλ1548,51, but no other metal lines [Tab. 4, p.11; Fig. 6, p.21]. This ratio exceeds even the hardest known local He II emitters.
  2. The revised oxygen abundance, 12+log(O/H)=6.08\(^{+0.24}_{-0.80}\) (0.04–0.4% solar), extrapolated from several strong-line R3 calibrations below their calibrated range, is the lowest of any galaxy of comparable mass, placing AMORE6 well below the mass-metallicity relation [Fig. 4, p.17]. C/O is close to or above the solar value, unusually high for such low O/H [§6.5, p.17; Fig. 5, p.19].
  3. Fig. 7 [p.23] shows the Bayesian ranking of ionizing-source families against the full line spectrum: four families are statistically indistinguishable (self-polluted and Pop III-rich NEFERTITI models, a top-heavy "Pop III.1" Salpeter IMF population, and a metal-poor population with X-ray binaries supplying ~20% of ionizing photons), while ordinary metal-poor populations, very massive stars, and massive-black-hole/AGN models are strongly disfavored.
  4. Under the fiducial self-polluted-Pop III SED fit, the system's Pop III mass is 1.3×10^5 M⊙, formed within the last 4 Myr, with 86% of ionizing photons escaping [p.24]. The He II/Balmer ratio jointly constrains the Pop III IMF slope x and characteristic mass Mch to 90x−268 ≲ Mch/M⊙ ≲ 172x−330 (3σ), excluding the most extreme top-heavy IMFs allowed by near-field (Milky Way halo) constraints [Abstract, p.1].

Caveats & limitations

The authors are explicit that strong-line metallicity calibrations are essentially uncalibrated below 12+log(O/H)≈7 and their extrapolation spans ~1 dex depending on the calibration used, which dominates the quoted abundance uncertainty [p.16]. The helium abundance rests on only a few faint He I lines, most below 3σ individually [p.14]. Emission lines alone cannot statistically separate the Pop III scenario from the X-ray-binary alternative [§7.4, p.23] — the paper leans on SED fitting and auxiliary X-ray luminosity arguments to disfavor the latter, noting it would require either implausibly young/luminous X-ray sources or ~2×10^6 M⊙ of older, metal-expelled Pop III.2 stars [Abstract]. My own observations: this is a single-object case study, so results shouldn't be over-generalized; and the inference chain depends on numerous technical corrections (lens magnification near a critical curve, slit/path-loss modeling, extended-range grating calibration) whose combined systematic effects are hard to fully assess from the summary alone.

Assessment

This is a careful, technically dense followup that meaningfully upgrades a tentative Pop III candidate into one of the most compelling cases to date, by replacing a single-line-ratio inference with multi-line, model-marginalized evidence — a methodologically strong approach given how degenerate single diagnostics are at these metallicities. It doesn't claim a definitive detection; the X-ray-binary alternative remains statistically live, which is an honest and relevant caveat. For someone working on stellar population synthesis and spectral fitting of galaxies, the extensive treatment of calibration degeneracies (R3 extrapolation, ionization/density effects on forbidden lines) and the Bayesian model-family comparison framework are directly relevant methodologically, even outside the Pop III context. Connects to Rusta, Salvadori and collaborators' NEFERTITI simulation framework, a group whose work is tracked.

Code & data

The line-fitting package unite is linked at https://github.com/TheSkyentist/unite [p.10, footnote]. Underlying imaging/spectroscopic datasets (UNCOVER DR3, JADES DR4, DJA archive) are public survey products referenced in the text, but no dedicated code/data repository for this paper's analysis is mentioned.

2. The ALMA REBELS Survey: multi-band dust continuum observations reveal heavily obscured starbursts among UV-bright galaxies at \(z\sim7\)

Hiddo Algera, Lucy Astles, Hanae Inami, Manuel Aravena, Tom Bakx, Rychard Bouwens et al. · astro-ph.GA · arXiv · PDF

Why it was picked: ALMA REBELS dust/SFR characterization of z~7 galaxies ties into galaxy evolution and obscured star formation, adjacent to their stellar-population/SFH interests. — score 5.2 (you 5/10 · field 6/10)

TL;DR. Using multi-band ALMA continuum photometry (including Band 8) for 13 massive Lyman-break galaxies at \(z\sim7\) from the REBELS survey and its follow-ups, the authors find dust temperatures of \(T_{\rm dust}\approx30-70\) K (stacked average \(49^{+11}_{-8}\) K), obscured star-formation fractions of \(f_{\rm obs}=0.75\pm0.11\), and dust-to-stellar mass ratios best matched by models with efficient interstellar dust growth — showing that heavily dust-obscured, IR-luminous starbursts are already common among UV-selected galaxies within the first ~700 Myr of cosmic history [p. 1].

Main question

Single-band ALMA continuum detections cannot independently constrain a galaxy's dust temperature, mass, and infrared luminosity, because these quantities are degenerate in the modified-blackbody fit — one must assume either a fixed temperature or a physically motivated scaling relation [§1, p. 2]. The paper asks: what are the actual dust temperatures, masses, and obscured star-formation rates of UV-bright galaxies at \(z\sim7\), once multi-band (two or more ALMA bands, including a high-frequency Band 8 point) data break this degeneracy? This matters because far-infrared/(sub)mm-selected dusty galaxies vanish from view beyond \(z\approx5\), so nearly everything known about dust in the reionization era comes from follow-up of UV-selected samples, and getting temperatures/masses right is essential for assessing cosmic obscured star-formation and early dust build-up pathways [§1, pp. 1–3].

Data & method

The sample is drawn from the Cycle 7 ALMA Large Program REBELS and seven ALMA follow-up programs, yielding 13 REBELS galaxies (10 "primary" UV-selected targets plus 3 serendipitous [C II]-detected companions) at \(z=6.5-7.7\), all observed in at least two ALMA bands, including Band 8 for all 13 [§2.2, p. 4; Table 1, p. 5]. In total this is 31 distinct observations from 8 ALMA projects [§7, p. 24]. The authors homogeneously re-image and calibrate all data, use PyBDSF for source detection, and adopt \((u,v)\)-plane exponential-disk model fitting as the fiducial flux-density measurement (cross-checked against peak, aperture, and 2D-Gaussian fluxes; Appendix C) [§4.1–4.2, pp. 6–11]. Dust SEDs are fit with an optically thin modified blackbody [Eq. 1, p. 11], fixing the emissivity index \(\beta_{\rm IR}=2.0\) as fiducial (with a Gaussian-prior variant in Appendix J). A key methodological innovation is a dust-temperature prior uniform in \(1/T_{\rm dust}\) rather than the commonly used flat prior, which the authors show (via mock fitting, Appendix G) substantially reduces temperature overestimation for low-S/N, two-band data [§4.3, p. 11]. They also construct inverse-variance-weighted stacks of the 10 primary targets in Band 6 and Band 8 jointly, in both image and \((u,v)\)-plane [§4.4, pp. 11–12].

Main results

  1. Dust temperatures. Individual fits span \(T_{\rm dust}\approx30-70\) K (median \(\approx44\) K); the stack gives a well-constrained \(T_{\rm dust}=49^{+11}_{-8}\) K, shown in Fig. 8 [p. 13] and in the redshift context of Fig. 9 [p. 16], where a linear fit to \(z>5\) LBGs finds no significant evidence for temperature evolution with redshift (slope differs from zero at only ~1.3\(\sigma\)).
  2. Heavily obscured star formation. The stack implies an average obscured fraction \(f_{\rm obs}=0.75\pm0.11\), with individual targets ranging \(f_{\rm obs}\approx0.4-0.95\) (Fig. 12, p. 18; Table 2, p. 14). Three sources (REBELS-18, -32, -38) formally qualify as ULIRGs (\(L_{\rm IR}>10^{12}\,L_\odot\)), implying SFR\(_{\rm UV+IR}\approx160-300\,M_\odot\,{\rm yr}^{-1}\); the estimated ULIRG fraction among dust-detected, multi-band LBGs is \(f_{\rm ULIRG}=0.32^{+0.19}_{-0.17}\) (Fig. 11, p. 17).
  3. Possible quiescent-galaxy progenitors. The reconstructed total SFRs of the most IR-luminous targets overlap the growth tracks reconstructed for some \(z\approx4-5\) quiescent galaxies, suggesting dusty, UV-bright \(z\sim7\) galaxies are plausible (partial) progenitors of this population (Fig. 14, p. 21).
  4. Dust-to-stellar mass ratios and build-up pathway. The sample shows \(M_{\rm dust}/M_\star=0.4^{+0.3}_{-0.2}\%\) (stack), which agrees best with simulations/semi-analytic models (dustyGadget, COLIBRE, the Santa Cruz SAM) where efficient ISM grain growth dominates dust build-up by \(z\approx7\), rather than models dominated by supernova dust production alone (Fig. 15, pp. 22–23).

Caveats & limitations

The authors explicitly note the sample is selection-biased: only REBELS galaxies already detected in dust continuum were followed up in additional bands, so results characterize an already dusty subset, not the general UV-bright LBG population [§4.1, p. 8; §6.2, p. 18]. Many individual temperature fits (6 of 12 sources) are prior-dominated given modest S/N (median S/N \(\approx4.4-4.9\sigma\), Fig. 5, p. 8), so individual \(T_{\rm dust}\), \(M_{\rm dust}\), \(L_{\rm IR}\) values carry large uncertainty. The fixed \(\beta_{\rm IR}=2.0\) assumption and literature comparison samples drawn from heterogeneous fitting methods add further uncertainty [§6, pp. 14–15]. No AGN diagnosis is possible due to lack of high-resolution spectroscopy [§6.2.3, p. 22]. My own observations: the quiescent-galaxy connection (Fig. 14) is explicitly only a qualitative overlay of growth tracks, not a number-density test, which the authors themselves flag as needed future work [p. 21]; and the comparison literature sample mixes different \(\beta_{\rm IR}\) priors and fitting conventions, which the paper itself cautions may bias comparisons [p. 15].

Assessment

This is a careful, incremental but methodologically useful observational paper: the main novelty is the systematic multi-band homogenization (more than doubling \(z>6.5\) Band 8 detections, from 6 to 14 [§7, p. 24]) and the inverse-temperature prior, which is a genuine, well-justified statistical improvement for low-S/N SED fitting that other practitioners in the field should probably adopt. For a researcher focused on stellar populations and full-spectrum fitting, the direct relevance is more about obscured-SFR systematics and dust-mass priors than about new modeling/ML techniques — there's no representation-learning or emulator component here, just careful Bayesian MBB fitting (emcee, nested sampling). The potential quiescent-galaxy progenitor link is the most exciting but least rigorously tested claim, and is worth watching for a follow-up with number-density comparisons.

Code & data

Not mentioned — no code or data repository link is given in the text; the analysis uses existing public tools (PyBDSF, CASA, emcee, uvplot, interferopy, linmix, PyMultiNest) [pp. 10–15].

3. Euclid: Discovery of a quasar at z = 8.13 hosting a 360-million-solar-mass black hole

J. Wolf, S. Belladitta, D. Yang, F. Guarneri, E. Ba\~nados, J. F. Hennawi et al. · astro-ph.GA · arXiv · PDF · 💻 code/data

Why it was picked: Flagship Euclid discovery of a z>8 quasar is a major result but centers on quasar/black-hole demographics, not galaxy stellar populations. — score 4.5 (you 3/10 · field 9/10)

TL;DR. Euclid imaging followed by a multi-facility spectroscopic campaign (Keck, HST, LBT, JWST) confirms EUCL J150452.291+625224.39 as a quasar at \(z_{sys}=8.129\pm0.005\), 630 Myr after the Big Bang, hosting a black hole of \(\log_{10}(M_{BH}/M_\odot)=8.33\)–\(8.89\) accreting at a comparatively low Eddington ratio of 0.12–0.44 [Abstract, p. 1].

Main question

The paper asks whether a luminous, "mature" broad-line quasar — with the classical spectral signatures seen at lower redshift — already existed at \(z>8\), and what that implies for how supermassive black holes (SMBHs) assembled hundreds of millions of solar masses within the first ~600 Myr of cosmic history [§1, p. 1]. This matters because each incremental push of the quasar redshift frontier tightens constraints on black-hole seeding and growth models, since there is less time available for conventional accretion to build such masses [§1, p. 1].

Data & method

The candidate was selected from Euclid Wide Survey (EWS) imaging (VIS \(I_E\) band plus NISP \(Y_E,J_E,H_E\) bands) over the 3644 deg\(^2\) available in August 2025, using an optical-dropout colour cut (\(I_E-J_E>3.05\)) combined with three independent quasar-selection pipelines (XDHZQSO extreme-deconvolution, an XGBoost classifier, and LePhare SED fitting), giving a mean photometric redshift \(\bar z_{phot}=8.13\pm0.10\) [§2.2, p. 2–3]. The source (photometry \(Y_E=24.10\pm0.16\), \(J_E=22.96\pm0.06\), \(H_E=22.67\pm0.06\), undetected in \(I_E\)) was then confirmed through a tiered spectroscopic campaign: Keck/MOSFIRE longslit (4800 s), HST/WFC3 G141 slitless spectroscopy, deep LBT/LUCI longslit spectroscopy (totalling tens of thousands of seconds), and finally JWST/NIRSpec fixed-slit spectroscopy with three gratings (G140H, G235H, G395M; 591–788 s each), summarized in Table 1 [p. 3]. Emission-line fitting used the Bayesian code unite, modeling the H\(\beta\)–[O III] and Mg II regions with power-law continuum, Fe II templates, and multi-component line profiles compared via Bayesian information criterion [§4.1, p. 6].

Main results

  1. Redshift and discovery spectrum. Fig. 3 [p. 7] shows the combined NIRSpec spectrum revealing a sharp Ly\(\alpha\) break and broad UV/optical lines (N V, C IV, C III], Mg II, H\(\beta\)), yielding \(z_{sys,nar}=8.129\pm0.005\) from the narrow H\(\beta\) component [§4.1, p. 6–7], surpassing the previous record (\(z=7.77\), Yang et al. 2026) by about 39 Myr, roughly 6% of the age of the Universe at that epoch [Abstract, p. 1].
  2. Black hole mass. From intrinsic FWHM(H\(\beta\),BLR)\(=3160^{+270}_{-270}\) km/s and FWHM(Mg II,BLR)\(=3740^{+160}_{-170}\) km/s (Fig. 4 [p. 8]), the benchmark Vestergaard & Peterson (2006) H\(\beta\) calibration gives \(\log_{10}(M_{BH}/M_\odot)=8.56^{+0.43}_{-0.43}\), with the full range across calibrations spanning 8.33–8.89 [Eq. 1–3, p. 7–8; Tab. 2, p. 9].
  3. Sub-Eddington accretion. Bolometric luminosity \(\log_{10}(L_{bol}/\text{erg s}^{-1})=46.08\pm0.02\) [Eq. 5, p. 9] implies Eddington ratios \(\lambda_{Edd}=0.12\)–0.44, placing the quasar toward the lower envelope of the known \(z>6\) quasar population, as shown in Fig. 5 [p. 9].
  4. Growth implications. Fig. 7 [p. 12] back-traces growth curves anchored to the measured mass: continuous Eddington-limited accretion (\(\epsilon=0.10\), \(f_{duty}=1\)) from \(z_{seed}=20\) requires a seed mass of order \(10^5\,M_\odot\) (a "massive seed"), while super-critical accretion scenarios permit seeds as low as Population III remnant masses (\(\sim10^2\,M_\odot\)) [§5, p. 11–12]. The authors state the discovery "does not enable a robust distinction between these formation pathways" [§5, p. 12].

Caveats & limitations

The authors explicitly note: the [O III] doublet is not formally detected (only a 3\(\sigma\) upper limit), so the systemic redshift relies on narrow H\(\beta\) rather than the generally preferred [O III] tracer, with a systematic uncertainty of 150 km/s adopted to cover narrow-line kinematics and uncertain low-\(z\) calibration applicability at this redshift [§4.1, p. 6–7]. Black hole mass estimates depend heavily on which single-epoch calibration is used (range 8.33–8.89 dex) [Eq. 3, p. 8], and these calibrations are extrapolated from much lower-redshift quasar samples. The source may be affected by a proximate damped Ly\(\alpha\) system complicating the Ly\(\alpha\)/C IV profile interpretation [§3.4, p. 6], and strong gravitational lensing cannot be fully excluded though deemed unlikely [§2.2, p. 3]. My own observation: the paper is a single-object discovery, so statistical inferences about the broader \(z>8\) population (e.g., "more luminous quasars may remain to be discovered," Abstract, p. 1) are necessarily speculative extrapolations from \(N=1\); the bolometric correction and virial black-hole-mass method also carry well-known systematic uncertainties (factor of several) beyond what is quoted, which the authors acknowledge only partially via the quoted calibration spread.

Assessment

This is a genuine discovery paper (redshift-record quasar) rather than a methods paper, so its primary interest to this researcher is more as a landmark data point than a transferable technique — though the multi-tiered selection/classification pipeline (XDHZQSO, XGBoost, SED fitting) and the Bayesian unite spectral-fitting framework are relevant examples of ML/Bayesian tools applied to large spectroscopic-survey data [§2.2, p. 2–3; §4.1, p. 6]. The result is reasonably convincing given the multi-facility, multi-epoch confirmation chain (Keck→HST→LBT→JWST) and the SDSS nearest-neighbour spectral comparison validating that the object looks like a genuine lower-\(z\) quasar analogue [Appendix A, p. 19]. The most scientifically striking point for stellar-population/galaxy-evolution-adjacent readers is the sub-Eddington accretion state at \(z>8\), which argues this SMBH already finished a rapid growth phase very early — directly relevant to questions about coevolution and early quenching/feedback physics, even though this paper itself does not analyze the host galaxy's stellar population (no [O II] detection, host-galaxy SFR analysis explicitly deferred, §4.1, p. 6).

Code & data

The unite (Unified liNe Integration Turbo Engine) fitting code is linked at https://github.com/TheSkyentist/unite/ [§4.1, p. 6]. Reduction software used includes PypeIt and Grizli (standard public tools, cited but not paper-specific repositories) [§3.1–3.4, p. 3–5]. No dedicated data-release repository for the spectra/catalogues themselves is mentioned.

4. The dust attenuation scaling relation in the TNG50 simulation

Pedro Cataldi, XianZhong Zheng, Antonios Katsianis, Sebastian Schulz, Man Qiao, Jianbo Qin et al. · astro-ph.GA · arXiv · PDF

This paper asks whether the TNG50 simulation (post-processed with SKIRT) reproduces the observed universal IRX-metallicity-size scaling relation for star-forming galaxies out to z~4. Comparing 7280 simulated galaxies to observations and EAGLE, they find good agreement at \(0.5\le z\le1\) but offsets of +0.5 dex at z=0 and -0.25 dex at z\ge2, partly resolved by lowering metallicities by ~0.2 dex or adjusting the dust-to-metal ratio.

Why you might care: Useful if you care about how dust/metallicity systematics in simulations could bias SED- or spectral-derived SFR and metallicity calibrations used in full-spectrum fitting. (score 4.2 (you 4/10 · field 5/10))

Abstract The infrared excess (IRX = L_IR / L_UV) of star-forming galaxies (SFGs) follows a tight scaling relation with star formation rate, gas-phase metallicity, and size up to z ~ 3, providing a key benchmark for galaxy formation models. We test whether TNG50 SFGs, post-processed with the radiative transfer code SKIRT, reproduce the universal IRX relation - linking IRX to metallicity, infrared luminosity, size, and inclination - and identify the origin of any offsets. We analyse 7280 TNG50-SFGs across 0 <= z <= 4, comparing their predicted IRX relations with observations and the EAGLE simulation. We test different dust-to-metal (DTM) ratios and dust compositions, recalibrate galaxy properties to observed scaling relations, and decompose the offsets into individual parameter contributions. TNG50-SFGs match the universal IRX relation closely at 0.5 <= z <= 1, but lie above it at z = 0 (+0.5 dex) and below it at z >= 2 (-0.25 dex). They follow a relation of the same functional form, with a shallower, metallicity-independent L_IR slope. At z ~ 0, TNG50-SFGs are ~1 dex fainter in L_IR than the local calibration sample, requiring extrapolation; at fixed stellar mass and L_IR, simulated and observed IRX agree within 0.04 dex. At z >= 2, TNG50-SFGs are less obscured than predicted, an offset largely removed by lowering their metallicities by ~0.2 dex. Varying the DTM ratio and dust type shifts the offset normalisation without resolving its redshift evolution: a redshift-dependent DTM ratio helps at high z, but not at z = 0. Resolving the low-z discrepancy requires both improved dust models and observational constraints on IR-faint galaxies.

5. Learning to find cosmic filaments with graph neural networks

Alexis Tastet, Daniela Gal\'arraga-Espinosa, Leander Thiele · astro-ph.CO · arXiv · PDF

The paper tackles reconstructing the cosmic web's filamentary structure from discrete galaxy catalogs, which is inherently uncertain. Using flow matching combined with graph neural networks, they generate a distribution of plausible filament configurations rather than a single deterministic structure, reproducing key statistics of the target filament network.

Why you might care: A generative/GNN approach to uncertainty-aware structure inference from sparse tracers is a methodological analogue worth skimming if you're interested in simulation-based inference or uncertainty quantification techniques transferable to spectroscopic property inference. (score 4.0 (you 4/10 · field 4/10))

Abstract Inferring the large-scale structure of the Universe from galaxy surveys is a fundamental challenge in cosmology. In particular, cosmic filaments form an extended and geometrically complex network whose underlying structure cannot be reconstructed from discrete galaxy catalogs without uncertainty. We investigate a generative approach to filament finding based on flow matching and graph neural networks. The learned model reproduces key statistical properties of the target filament distribution while generating a distribution over possible filament configurations, demonstrating its ability to model uncertainty. Our work provides a first step towards probabilistic inference of the cosmic web directly from galaxy surveys. More broadly, these results demonstrate the potential of conditional generative models for reconstructing filament-like structures from sparse observational tracers.

6. VENUS: A Census of Strong Lensing in a 60-Cluster JWST Survey

Joseph F. V. Allingham, Lukas J. Furtak, Miriam Golubchik, Ashish K. Meena, Adi Zitrin, John Chisholm et al. · astro-ph.CO · astro-ph.GA · arXiv · PDF · cross

This paper presents strong-lensing models for 57 of 60 JWST VENUS galaxy clusters, totaling 5013 lensed images (1733 systems, 912 with spectroscopic redshifts), achieving 0.54'' image-plane precision and establishing the largest homogeneous cluster lensing survey to date, with scaling relations between lensing observables and cluster mass.

Why you might care: Mostly a cluster-lensing/high-z magnification resource; only tangential to galaxy spectroscopy/stellar populations unless you need lensed high-z spectroscopic targets. (score 4.0 (you 3/10 · field 7/10))

Abstract We present strong-lensing models for the 57 galaxy clusters observed in Cycle 4 out of the 60 targeted by the JWST Vast Exploration for Nascent, Unexplored Sources (VENUS) program. Models for the remaining three clusters will be incorporated after their observations. The dataset comprises 5013 individual images, corresponding to 1733 multiply-imaged systems, of which 912 have spectroscopic redshift measurements and 227 are newly identified. Using this new wealth of observations, we construct parametric lens models with our $\mathtt{AstroLensPy}$ software, reproducing the observed image positions with an average image-plane precision $\Delta_{\rm RMS}$ of $0.54''$. With a total imaging area of $576$ arcmin$^2$, VENUS offers the largest homogeneous set of cluster strong-lensing models to date. The clusters yield a combined area of $191$ arcmin$^{2}$ magnified by $|\mu|\geq3$ for a source $z_s=10$, for example, establishing the VENUS fields as a premier survey for high-redshift studies. Among our sample, the clusters MACS J0717.5+3745, RXC J0600.1-2007 and PLCK G287.0+32.9 display the largest Einstein radii ($\geq 60''$) and magnified areas ($\mathcal{A}_{z_s=10}^{|\mu|\geq3} \geq 9$ arcmin$^2$) for a source $z_s=10$, and therefore constitute the most potent gravitational telescopes known to date. Finally, leveraging the statistical power of the sample, we derive scaling relations between various lensing observables (Einstein radius, magnified area, projected mass) and the SZ/X-ray-inferred cluster mass, $M_{500,c}$.

7. Symbolic Regression Diagnostics of 1D Lyman Alpha Forest Emulator

Aidan Behmer, Ming-Feng Ho, Camille Avestruz, Yanhui Yang, Simeon Bird · astro-ph.CO · arXiv · PDF

The authors use symbolic regression to interpret a black-box Gaussian-process emulator of the Lyman-alpha forest power spectrum, finding that equations matching emulator values well don't necessarily preserve accurate parameter derivatives. Introducing a Sobolev (derivative-matching) loss, they recover derivative-faithful equations for 9 of 11 cosmological/astrophysical parameters at z=3.6.

Why you might care: A methodological lesson on emulator interpretability and derivative-faithfulness that's directly transferable to building trustworthy ML emulators for spectral fitting pipelines. (score 3.2 (you 3/10 · field 4/10))

Abstract We use symbolic regression to diagnose the parameter dependence of a black-box multi-fidelity emulator of the Lyman-alpha forest 1D flux power spectrum. Symbolic regression is appealing in its ability to reduce complex parameter spaces, thereby reducing compute time and offering potential interpretability in parameter inter-dependencies. Starting from a pretrained Gaussian-process (GP) emulator that connects low- and high-resolution PRIYA simulations, we search for explicit equations describing the dependence of the power spectrum on resolution and each cosmological and astrophysical parameter. We find that symbolic regression can recover equations that reproduce the emulator values well, but accurate predictions do not guarantee accurate parameter derivatives, making such equations unreliable for downstream applications such as Fisher forecasts. We therefore introduce a derivative-faithfulness diagnostic that directly compares the parameter gradient of each symbolic equation to the GP, and explore a derivative-matching (Sobolev) loss to improve this agreement.
