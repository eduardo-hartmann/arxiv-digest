### 1. Environmental dependence of the properties of H$\alpha$ emitters in the extremely massive galaxy protoclusters BOSS1244 and BOSS1542 at $z = 2.24$

*Dongdong Shi (AUST), XianZhong Zheng (SJTU), Zheng Cai (Tsinghua), Xiaohui Fan (UA), Xin Wang (UCAS), Helmut Dannerbauer (IAC) et al.* · `astro-ph.GA` · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30054) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.30054)

> **Why it was picked:** Environmental dependence of Halpha emitter properties (mass, SFR, color) in protoclusters — directly touches galaxy evolution/quenching via spectroscopic SFR tracers. — score **5.8** (you 6/10 · field 5/10)

**TL;DR.** Using narrowband-Hα-selected emitter catalogs in two extremely massive z=2.24 protoclusters, the authors find that color– and stellar-mass–density relations are already in place at this redshift in one system (BOSS1244) but essentially absent in the other (BOSS1542), arguing that environmental effects on galaxies depend on a structure's dynamical/assembly stage rather than appearing uniformly once a density threshold is crossed [Abstract, p. 1].

#### Main question
The paper asks whether environmental effects on galaxy properties — analogous to the morphology–, color–, and SFR–density relations well established at $z\lesssim1$ — are already operating at $z>2$ on protocluster scales ($>30$ cMpc), and whether the strength of these effects depends on how evolved/virialized the large-scale structure is [§1, p. 1-2]. This matters because studies of individual high-$z$ protoclusters give contradictory answers (some show quenched massive galaxies, others enhanced star formation or mergers), and the authors argue this scatter may reflect genuine differences in assembly state rather than measurement inconsistency [§1, p. 2].

#### Data & method
The targets are BOSS1244 and BOSS1542, two $z=2.24\pm0.02$ protoclusters with galaxy overdensity $\delta_g=5.6\pm0.3$ and $4.9\pm0.3$ over $\sim$39 cMpc, each with total enclosed mass $\sim10^{16}\,M_\odot$ — among the most massive protoclusters known at $z=2-3$ [§2.1, p. 3]. Hα emitters (HAEs) were selected using CFHT/WIRCam narrowband H2S1 and broadband Ks imaging, with contaminants removed via $BzsKs$, $UBzs$, and (for BOSS1244, which lacks B-band) $UzsKs$ color cuts [Eqs. 1-7, §3.1, p. 5]. This yields 207 HAEs in BOSS1244 and 151 in BOSS1542 after color selection, out of 244/223 original candidates [Tab. 2, p. 6]; subsets have NIR spectroscopic or HST grism redshifts (55 and 37, respectively) [Tab. 2, p. 6]. Stellar masses are estimated not via SED fitting (insufficient photometric bands) but from Ks-band luminosity and a theoretical mass-to-light ratio calibrated by Koyama et al. (2013a) [Eq. 8, §3.2, p. 6]. SFRs come from Hα flux (via H2S1/Ks photometry), corrected for [NII] contamination and for dust using the local $A_{H\alpha}$–$M_*$ relation of Garn & Best (2010), then converted with the Kennicutt (1998) calibration [Eqs. 9-12, §3.3, p. 6-7]. Local environment is quantified with the projected fifth-nearest-neighbor surface density $\Sigma_{5th}$ [§3.4, p. 7], benchmarked against field HAEs in COSMOS/UDS/ECDFS [Tab. 3, p. 7].

#### Main results
1. **Color–magnitude and color–density relations differ sharply between fields.** In BOSS1244, HAEs in high-density regions are redder ($z_s-K_s=1.53\pm0.11$ vs $1.31\pm0.13$) and brighter in Ks than outskirt HAEs [Fig. 4, p. 7-8]; the color–$\Sigma_{5th}$ correlation is weak but statistically significant (Spearman $r=0.28$, $p=6.0\times10^{-5}$). In BOSS1542, high- and low-density HAE colors are indistinguishable ($1.44\pm0.09$ vs $1.50\pm0.14$), and the density correlation is not significant ($r=0.10$, $p=0.21$) [§4.3.1, Fig. 6, p. 10]. A similar, somewhat weaker mass–density trend appears in BOSS1244 ($r=0.21$, $p=2.0\times10^{-3}$) but is marginal in BOSS1542 [§4.3.2, Fig. 6, p. 10].
2. **Red HAEs ($z_s-K_s>2.5$) are a massive, overdense, morphologically disturbed population.** 28 are found in BOSS1244 and 19 in BOSS1542, concentrated in the densest cores, with median $\log(M_*/M_\odot)=11.20\pm0.09$ and $11.05\pm0.13$ respectively — roughly a full dex above the bluer HAE population [Tab. 4, Fig. 3, p. 7-9]. Their surface-density overdensity relative to COSMOS is $6.60\pm1.80$ (BOSS1244) and $4.45\pm1.18$ (BOSS1542) [§4.2, p. 9]. HST F160W cutouts show most are irregular or multi-component rather than compact/quiescent [Fig. 5, p. 9], and one extremely massive ($>10^{12}M_\odot$) red HAE in BOSS1244-SW is detected at 850 µm, confirming dust obscuration in at least some cases [§4.2, p. 9-10].
3. **The star-forming main sequence (SFMS) holds in both fields but with different slopes.** Fig. 7 [p. 11] shows HAEs in both protoclusters broadly tracking literature SFMS relations, but BOSS1542's best-fit slope ($0.76\pm0.01$) is steeper than BOSS1244's ($0.62\pm0.01$), meaning massive galaxies in BOSS1542 form stars more vigorously relative to their mass [§4.4, p. 11-12].
4. **Radial Hα equivalent-width (EW, a proxy for specific SFR) gradients are inside-out in the densest cores.** In BOSS1244-SW, EW rises from center to outskirts (Spearman $r=0.5$, $p=0.009$), consistent with earlier evidence of inside-out quenching around a pair of quiescent galaxies there; BOSS1542-Filament shows a similar rise away from a central quasar ($r=0.37$, $p=0.046$); BOSS1244-NE shows a weak, marginal decline ($r=-0.44$, $p=0.05$) [Fig. 8, §4.5, p. 12-13].

#### Caveats & limitations
The authors note: NB flux-limit incompleteness may bias the sample toward high-EW galaxies; stellar masses rely on a single-color M/L calibration rather than full SED fitting (though validated against SED fitting by Koyama et al. 2013a for similar samples); the dust correction is calibrated on local galaxies and extrapolated to $z\sim2$; and spectroscopic confirmation is incomplete and currently restricted to the densest sub-regions [§6, p. 15-16]. The authors also explicitly caution that correlation coefficients (Spearman/Pearson) establish statistical association, not causation, and that differing coefficients across fields do not directly rank the strength of environmental effects because they depend on sample size, dynamic range, and scatter [§4.3.2, p. 10-11]. My own additions: the red-HAE and mass–density samples are small (tens of objects, often with only 3-7 spectroscopic redshifts each), the field-comparison samples (COSMOS/UDS/ECDFS) use slightly different NB filters/depths than BOSS1244/1542 [§3.4, p. 7], and photometric redshifts for most of the sample are uncertain (only $\gtrsim$70% fall at $z_{\rm phot}=1.6-2.4$) [§3.1, p. 5].

#### Assessment
This is a solid, incremental observational paper rather than a methodological one — no ML or spectral-fitting machinery is used; masses and SFRs come from simple photometric scaling relations rather than full-spectrum or SED modeling, which is a real limitation given the researcher's interest in rigorous stellar-population inference. Its value is the controlled comparison: two protoclusters matched in redshift, mass, and overdensity but in different dynamical states give opposite answers to "does environment affect galaxies at $z>2$?" — a clean empirical case for assembly-state-dependent quenching onset, echoing the PKS1138/USS1558 dichotomy discussed in §5.2 [p. 14]. The statistics are modest (several correlations are only marginally significant), so the "accelerated evolution" narrative is plausible but not airtight. Most relevant to this researcher's interests in quenching and environment-dependent stellar populations from spectroscopy, though the spectroscopic sample here is small and used mainly for redshift confirmation, not population synthesis.

#### Code & data
Not mentioned. The appendix provides coordinate/property tables for the red HAE samples (Tables A.1, A.2, p. 18), but no software or public data repository links appear in the text.

### 2. Discovery of Two Hyperluminous Infrared Galaxies at $z\sim6$

*Shuowen Jin, Georgios E. Magdis, Raphael Gobat, Eva Schinnerer, Emanuele Daddi, Herv\'e Dole et al.* · `astro-ph.GA` · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29560) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.29560)

> **Why it was picked:** High-z hyperluminous galaxy spectroscopic confirmation with SED/SFR properties, touches galaxy evolution but via dust/CO not stellar population synthesis. — score **5.5** (you 5/10 · field 7/10)

**TL;DR.** The authors spectroscopically confirm two unlensed hyperluminous infrared galaxies (HyLIRGs) at $z\sim6$ using ALMA and NOEMA CO/[C II] line detections, finding SFRs of ~1000–2300 $M_\odot\,\mathrm{yr}^{-1}$, extreme [C II] deficits, and gas depletion times of only ~40–60 Myr, making them candidate progenitors of the earliest massive quiescent galaxies seen near $z\sim5$ [p. 1].

#### Main question
The paper asks whether two dusty, millimeter-selected sources in COSMOS are genuine $z\sim6$ HyLIRGs ($L_{\rm IR}\geq10^{13}L_\odot$), and what their star-formation rates, sizes, and interstellar medium conditions imply for the formation of the most massive early galaxies. This matters because HyLIRGs are candidate progenitors of compact quiescent galaxies and massive ellipticals, galaxy formation models struggle to produce such luminous dusty starbursts only ~1 Gyr after the Big Bang, and JWST has recently found abundant massive quiescent galaxies at $z\gtrsim3$–5 implying formation epochs at $z\gtrsim6$–10, requiring a population of vigorous, dust-obscured starbursts to explain them [§1, p. 1].

#### Data & method
The two targets, AzTECC63 (from the ASTE/AzTEC 1.1 mm survey) and S2COS-84 (from JCMT/SCUBA-2 850 µm survey), were originally selected in COSMOS and had candidate redshifts $z\gtrsim6$ from FIR SEDs and archival ALMA data [§2.1, p. 2]. The authors obtained new ALMA 3 mm line scans plus NOEMA/ALMA follow-up to detect CO and [C II] 158 µm lines, reduced with CASA and GILDAS, with spectra extracted via uv-plane fitting [§2.2, p. 2]. Panchromatic SED fitting used the MICHI2 code with five components (stellar, mid-IR AGN, warm/cold dust, radio) [§3.3, p. 3], and dust properties were derived from optically thin and general-opacity modified-blackbody fits including CMB effects [§3.3, p. 4]. For S2COS-84, high-resolution ALMA Band 7 data allowed uv-plane size fitting with an elliptical Spergel profile [§3.2, p. 3]. Neither source shows evidence of lensing (magnification $\mu<1.04$) [§2.3, p. 2].

#### Main results
Robust redshifts were confirmed: $z=6.092$ for AzTECC63 (CO(6–5) and [C II] at $>6\sigma$) and $z=5.90$ for S2COS-84 (CO(5–4), CO(6–5), [C II]), shown in Figs. 1 and 2 [p. 3–4]. SED fitting gives $\log(L_{\rm IR}/L_\odot)=13.02\pm0.03$ and $13.36\pm0.04$, corresponding to $\mathrm{SFR_{IR}}\approx1000$ and $2300\,M_\odot\,\mathrm{yr}^{-1}$ [§3.3, Tab. 1, p. 4], ranking them among the most intensely star-forming $z\gtrsim6$ systems known, comparable only to the lensed SPT0311−58 and obscured quasars COS-87259 and GNz7q [§3.3, p. 4]. S2COS-84 is extremely compact, with dust-continuum effective radius $R_e=0.28\pm0.01$ kpc (Spergel/Sérsic index $n\approx1.3$) while [C II] is $2.7\pm0.3\times$ more extended ($R_e=0.75\pm0.07$ kpc) [§3.2, p. 3], implying $\Sigma_{\rm SFR}\approx4700\,M_\odot\,\mathrm{yr}^{-1}\,\mathrm{kpc}^{-2}$, more than two orders of magnitude above typical DSFGs and above the Eddington limit (~$10^3\,M_\odot\,\mathrm{yr}^{-1}\,\mathrm{kpc}^{-2}$) [§4, p. 5]. Both galaxies show an extreme [C II] deficit, $L_{\rm [CII]}/L_{\rm IR}\approx10^{-3.7}$, about an order of magnitude below normal star-forming galaxies, shown against literature samples in Fig. 3 [p. 5]. Molecular gas masses from [C II] agree well with dust-based estimates ($\log(M_{\rm mol}/M_\odot)=10.66\pm0.17$ and $11.10\pm0.12$), and combined with the IR SFRs imply short depletion times $\tau_{\rm dep}\approx40$–60 Myr [§3.4, p. 4–5], meaning gas reservoirs would be exhausted by $z\approx5.6$–5.8 absent replenishment, supporting a link to the earliest massive quiescent galaxies at $z\gtrsim4$–5 [§3.4, p. 5].

#### Caveats & limitations
The authors note AzTECC63 lacks high-resolution imaging, so its compactness and optically thick dust interpretation remain unconfirmed [§3.3, p. 4; §5, p. 6]. For S2COS-84's [C II] line, the red wing is partly missing (band edge), so flux relies on a CO-profile-based correction with propagated uncertainty [§3.1, p. 3]. The [C II] deficit cannot be fully explained by dust opacity alone; the authors explicitly discuss that the remainder requires either extreme ISM conditions (thermal saturation, reduced photoelectric heating, ionizing-photon absorption in H II regions) or a dust-obscured AGN contributing up to ≳80% of $L_{\rm IR}$, which current data cannot distinguish — JWST mid-IR observations are called for [§4, p. 5–6]. Stellar mass for AzTECC63 is only an upper limit ($M_*<4.6\times10^{10}M_\odot$) due to non-detection in IRAC/optical/NIR [§3.3, p. 4]. My own observation: with only two objects, number-density/statistical constraints on galaxy formation models are not addressed, and the depletion-time argument assumes no gas replenishment, which the authors flag as a simplifying assumption [§3.4, p. 5] rather than a demonstrated fact.

#### Assessment
This is a solid, data-driven discovery paper rather than a methods or large-sample study — its value is in two carefully confirmed, unlensed $z\sim6$ HyLIRGs, which are rare (the authors note only a handful of confirmed $z\gtrsim5$ systems, mostly lensed) [§1, p. 2]. The size/compactness and [C II]-deficit analysis is the most interesting physical result and connects well to quenching narratives relevant to this researcher's interests: short depletion times and super-Eddington $\Sigma_{\rm SFR}$ make a reasonably convincing (though not airtight) case these are progenitors of early massive quiescent galaxies, tying dusty-starburst spectroscopy directly to the JWST-discovered population of $z\sim4$–5 quiescent galaxies. It is less directly about stellar population synthesis or ML, but the panchromatic SED fitting (MICHI2) and systematic size/gas-mass derivations are methodologically relevant to full-spectrum/SED-fitting work on large surveys. The AGN-vs-starburst ambiguity for S2COS-84 is honestly flagged rather than resolved, which is appropriately cautious given the data in hand.

#### Code & data
Not mentioned (no code or data repository links given in the text; MICHI2 and GILDAS/CASA are cited as external tools/pipelines, not linked).

### 3. JAISP: A Joint Rubin-Euclid Foundation Model for Cross-Instrument Source Detection and Astrometry

*Shoubaneh Hemmati, George Helou, Yu-Heng Lin, Rachel Akeson, Charles Darren Dowell, Andreas Faisst et al.* · `astro-ph.IM` · `astro-ph.CO` · `astro-ph.GA` · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31204) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.31204) · _cross_

> **Why it was picked:** Foundation model for joint Rubin-Euclid imaging source detection — ML/representation-learning methodology relevant as a transferable technique, though applied to imaging not spectra. — score **5.5** (you 5/10 · field 7/10)

**TL;DR.** The authors build Jaisp, a self-supervised "foundation model" that learns a single ten-band representation of overlapping Rubin and Euclid imaging, then use frozen features from it to drive lightweight detection and astrometry networks that beat or match classical single-survey pipelines, with the detection gain and astrometric transfer both holding on a second, never-seen deep field [p. 1].

#### Main question
The paper asks whether the joint pixel content of two independent imaging surveys — Rubin (ground-based, optical ugrizy) and Euclid (space-based VIS plus NISP Y, J, H) — can be distilled, without labels, into one learned representation precise enough to drive real measurements: source detection and astrometry [p. 1, §1]. This matters because precision cosmology (weak lensing, photo-z) is now limited by systematics rather than statistics, and combining surveys at the pixel level — rather than after each pipeline has produced its own catalog — was argued by an earlier "Joint Survey Processing" study to be the way to suppress survey-specific systematics, provided the surveys first achieve a common astrometric/photometric "concordance frame" [p. 1-2].

#### Data & method
Training data are Rubin Data Preview 1 (ugrizy, commissioning-quality, ~1″ FWHM) and Euclid Quick Release 1 (VIS at 0.1″ and NISP Y/J/H), both drawn from the overlapping Euclid Deep Field Fornax/ECDFS region, tiled into 790 matched 512×512-pixel cutouts over ≈0.2 deg² [p. 2-4, Fig. 1 p. 3]. A second, widely separated field (EDF-S, 72 tiles, ≈0.02 deg²) is held out entirely for an out-of-distribution transfer test [p. 2].

The foundation model is a masked-band autoencoder: one of the ten bands is withheld and predicted from the other nine, with no labels used [§3, p. 4]. Architecture: per-band convolutional "stems," two parallel ConvNeXt encoder branches (Rubin vs Euclid) that reduce to a common 0.4″/pixel grid, a 4-layer/8-head/256-d transformer bottleneck that fuses the two streams, and per-instrument decoders — about 9.1M parameters total, trained in ~8 GPU-hours on a single L40S [§3, p. 4-6, Fig. 2 p. 5]. The authors explicitly report that contrastive/JEPA-style representation-alignment objectives failed because they don't force the network to preserve sub-pixel source position, which detection and astrometry need; pixel reconstruction was adopted instead for this reason [§3.1, p. 6]. Once trained, the encoder is frozen; small task heads (<1M parameters each) are trained on its cached features for detection (CenterNet-style peak-finding, 0.55M params) and astrometry (0.7M params, anchored to a classical VIS Gaussian centroid) [§4-5, p. 9, p. 17].

#### Main results
1. **Representation validity.** Held-out bands are reconstructed with correlation 0.64-0.99 depending on band depth/sharpness [Fig. 3, p. 7]; per-band brightness is linearly decodable from frozen features ($R^2\approx0.7$-$0.95$) [Fig. 4 left, p. 8]; gradient-attribution analysis shows the model routes information between bands in physically sensible ways, e.g. NISP bands drawing mainly on each other [Fig. 4 right, p. 8-9].
2. **Detection.** The fused-feature head matches classical VIS-based source extraction and, by combining all ten bands, extends the point-source 50%-completeness depth by ≈0.3 mag (VIS≈26.7 vs. 26.4 for VIS-only), with the gain traced specifically to the fused bottleneck features (a stem-level variant shows no gain) [Fig. 6, Tab. 2, p. 10, 13]. It recovers ≈85-97% of the clean Euclid MER reference catalog [§4.5, p. 14].
3. **Astrometry.** The head transfers VIS's sharp localization to the other nine bands, collapsing raw ≈45-55 mas cross-band positional scatter to 6-10 mas medians [Fig. 7-8, p. 15-16], and injection tests against known truth positions give ≈17 mas error at S/N=5, within ~1 mas of the ≈16 mas VIS-anchor floor itself [Fig. 9, p. 17].
4. **Concordance field.** After removing per-source noise, a coherent 9-10 mas systematic offset remains between the two independently Gaia-registered astrometric solutions, ~7 mas of it common to all ten bands — i.e. a geometric registration offset between the two surveys' delivered frames rather than per-band or chromatic noise [Fig. 10, p. 18]. Applied with no retraining to EDF-S, both detection (94.5% vs. 93.9% completeness) and astrometry (same 6-10 mas floor) reproduce in-distribution performance [Fig. 11, p. 21].

#### Caveats & limitations
The authors are explicit that: detection remains VIS-anchored (other bands add depth only in combination with VIS, rarely drive detections alone) [p. 19, 22]; the astrometry head's accuracy is bounded by, and essentially saturates, the classical VIS-centroid labels it is trained against, so it transfers VIS precision rather than improving on it or on the Gaia tie [§5.3, p. 17-18]; the EDF-S transfer test probes a new sky patch with identical instruments/processing, not new hardware — cross-instrument transfer (e.g. to Roman) is untested [p. 22]; and all results come from the earliest, shallow, commissioning-quality public releases (Rubin DP1, Euclid Q1) over a single Rubin tract, a small fraction of the eventual survey overlap [§8, p. 21-22]. My own observation: the concordance-field decomposition relies on only two deep fields and coarse (5′-15′) spatial scales, so its generalization to the full survey footprint is, by the authors' own admission, unverified [§5.4, p. 19].

#### Assessment
This is an imaging/astrometry paper, not a spectroscopy one, so it's tangential to stellar-population work, but it's a clean, well-controlled demonstration of self-supervised masked-reconstruction pretraining (MAE-style) applied across heterogeneous instruments, with careful ablations (reconstruction vs. contrastive objectives, linear-probe decodability, gradient attribution) that would translate directly to building spectroscopic foundation models across surveys (e.g., DESI/SDSS/4MOST cross-calibration) — relevant to the "foundation models for astronomy" and "representation learning on spectra" interests. The negative result on contrastive/JEPA objectives failing to preserve position is a useful methodological lesson for anyone doing cross-survey or cross-instrument representation learning. The result is presented at small scale (one tract, two fields) and the authors are careful not to overclaim; it reads as a credible proof-of-concept rather than an operational pipeline.

#### Code & data
Code, training recipes, and figure notebooks are public at https://github.com/xoubish/JAISP (BSD-3-clause); trained weights, detection lists, and corrected positions are released under CC-BY 4.0 and archived on Zenodo (doi:10.5281/zenodo.23598660) [§7, p. 20].

### 4. Deep UV Spectroscopy of an Extremely Metal-Poor Galaxy at z = 5.725

*Takahiro Morishita, Massimo Stiavelli, Zhaoran Liu, Matteo Messa, Eros Vanzella* · `astro-ph.GA` · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30384) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.30384)

This paper asks whether AMORE6, a galaxy at z=5.725, is truly extremely metal-poor or could instead be dense, chemically enriched gas mimicking that signature. Deep X-shooter spectroscopy rules out the enriched-gas scenario via non-detection of CIV, and a narrow symmetric Lyman-alpha line ($\sigma_v = 23.2$ km/s) combined with source size yields a dynamical mass of $(1-22)\times10^7\,M_\odot$, while SED fitting with Pop III/Pop II libraries gives stellar masses of $(1-5)\times10^5\,M_\odot$. No model simultaneously reproduces the observed H$\beta$ luminosity and equivalent width, with observations exceeding predictions by up to 3.7$\sigma$.

**Why you might care:** A clear case where standard stellar population/recombination templates fail to fit an extreme, metal-poor object, relevant to anyone pushing full-spectrum fitting methods to their physical limits. <small>(score **5.2** (you 5/10 · field 6/10))</small>

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

We present deep X-shooter follow-up spectroscopy of AMORE6, an exceptionally metal-poor galaxy at z = 5.725. The new spectrum provides stringent upper limits on rest-frame ultraviolet metal lines, including NV, CIV, NIV], NIII], and OIII]. The non-detection of CIV breaks the density-metallicity degeneracy present in previous analyses based on the [OIII]/Hbeta ratio, excluding the high-density, chemically enriched gas scenario and strengthening the evidence for an extremely metal-poor environment. The X-shooter spectrum also reveals a narrow Lyman-alpha line with a velocity dispersion sigma_v = 23.2 +/- 0.5 km/s and a nearly symmetric profile centered at the systemic redshift measured from Hbeta. Combining the Lyman-alpha line width with the measured source size, we infer a dynamical mass M_dyn = (1-22) x 10^7 Msun, assuming that the line width traces gravitational motions.
