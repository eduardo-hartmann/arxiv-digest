# Learned preferences

## More of
- Large-survey spectroscopic catalogs that derive stellar population properties, kinematics, and emission-line measurements for big galaxy samples (e.g., LAMOST galaxy parameter catalog) — squarely in core interests and praised as "methodologically sound and interesting."
- Unsupervised / representation-learning approaches applied to galaxy evolution questions (e.g., clustering methods uncovering physical pathways in simulations) — aligns with core ML-on-spectra interest. (tentative, one data point)

## Less of
- Survey/instrument performance and data-quality characterization papers (e.g., redshift measurement performance reports) — even for surveys the researcher follows (Euclid), these rank lower unless they contain a notable scientific result beyond pipeline/performance validation.

## Instructions from the researcher
- For major survey papers that are primarily pipeline/performance/data-release characterizations rather than new science results, a short summary is enough — don't surface them as high-priority reads even if the survey itself is on the core list.

## Calibration notes
- Big-name surveys (Euclid, DESI, SDSS/MaNGA, LAMOST, etc.) being in the core interests list does not mean every paper from them is high value — distinguish "science result" / methodologically-sound catalog papers (rank high) from "performance/validation/release" papers (rank low, summary-only). Confirmed again by the LAMOST catalog +1, which is a methods-and-results paper on stellar populations/kinematics from survey spectra, not a pipeline-performance report.
- Data-driven/unsupervised methods papers on galaxy formation score well even without a direct spectroscopy angle, as long as they speak to physical pathways/galaxy evolution. (tentative, one data point)
- Papers that combine large-survey spectroscopy with derived physical properties (stellar populations, kinematics, emission lines) and are judged "methodologically sound" tend to rate highly — this is a good proxy signal for quality within the core topic area.
