# Learned preferences

## More of
- Unsupervised / representation-learning approaches applied to galaxy evolution questions (e.g., clustering methods uncovering physical pathways in simulations) — aligns with core ML-on-spectra interest. (tentative, one data point)

## Less of
- Survey/instrument performance and data-quality characterization papers (e.g., redshift measurement performance reports) — even for surveys the researcher follows (Euclid), these rank lower unless they contain a notable scientific result beyond pipeline/performance validation.

## Instructions from the researcher
- For major survey papers that are primarily pipeline/performance/data-release characterizations rather than new science results, a short summary is enough — don't surface them as high-priority reads even if the survey itself is on the core list.

## Calibration notes
- Big-name surveys (Euclid, DESI, SDSS/MaNGA, etc.) being in the core interests list does not mean every paper from them is high value — distinguish "science result" papers (rank high) from "performance/validation/release" papers (rank low, summary-only). (tentative, based on one Euclid example — watch for whether this generalizes to other surveys)
- Data-driven/unsupervised methods papers on galaxy formation score well even without a direct spectroscopy angle, as long as they speak to physical pathways/galaxy evolution. (tentative, one data point)
